### [The Unequal Weight of Genes: How Early-Life Environments Modify the Effects of Genetic Predispositions](https://github.com/ShutianLiuinfinite/gbye-mcs-bmises-public/raw/main/paper.pdf)

<div class="meta">Shutian Liu &middot; 2026</div>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div class="abstract" markdown="1">
Children from poorer families show a stronger link between genetic predictors and body weight. The dominant interpretation is that disadvantaged environments amplify children's own genetic risk for obesity. Previous research has relied on standard polygenic indices, which conflate the child's directly inherited genetic effects with the effects of parents' genotypes operating through the household environment, and thus has been unable to disentangle the two channels. I use linked parent&ndash;child genotypes from 5,446 children in the UK Millennium Cohort Study to compare a standard polygenic index for body mass index (from a 700,000-individual population GWAS) with a direct-effects polygenic index (from an 82,000-family GWAS that isolates inherited variation). The standard index reproduces the documented socioeconomic gradient; the direct-effects index's gradient is statistically indistinguishable from zero. Contrary to the dominant interpretation, a Hausman-style restriction test rules out the reading that the gradient operates through children's own genetic response to environment&mdash;it operates through family-level channels: parental genotypes and the household environments they shape. The same comparison applied to mental health and smoking outcomes establishes that the BMI pattern is trait-specific, not a mechanical artefact of the design. The findings overturn the standard policy implication that disadvantaged children should be screened by genetic risk; the lever for the BMI gradient lies in the household environment through which parental genetic background operates.
</div>
</details>

### Lower Endowment, Heavier Toll: Polygenic Moderation of the Cyberbullying&ndash;Mental-Health Gradient in Adolescence

<div class="meta">Shutian Liu &middot; 2026</div>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div class="abstract" markdown="1">
Adolescent mental health in Europe is deteriorating, and the cross-sectional relationship between cyberbullying and adolescent mental health is widely documented. Public health discussions treat this relationship as uniform across adolescents, but the conditional association may vary systematically with adolescents' genetic predispositions. This project asks whether the age-17 SDQ&ndash;cyberbullying association in the Millennium Cohort Study varies with the cohort member's polygenic index for educational attainment. I estimate gene-by-environment specifications on a both-parents-genotyped trio sample, with mother's and father's edu PGI, value-added controls, and parental mental health as covariates. The PGI moderation parameter is identified within-family via random meiotic-draw variation in the cohort member's PGI conditional on both parents' PGI. Preliminary results indicate that the conditional SDQ&ndash;cyberbullying association is roughly three times steeper at the bottom of the polygenic-index distribution than at the top, with the gradient concentrated among girls and steeper in emotional and peer subscales than in conduct or hyperactivity.
</div>
</details>
